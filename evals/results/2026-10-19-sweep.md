# 2026-10-19: screening every message of the person's (the sweep)

What `ask` changed after 0.3.1, and what the changes measured. The question behind them: `ask`
chooses search strings and reads only the records that hold them, the newest 60 of the
person's and 40 of the agents' for each string. On a real history that is a small part of
what bears on a question (one string held by 2,334 agent records gave 40; an earlier analysis
read 77 of 958 candidates), and a message of the person's in other words than the strings is
never seen. But the person's own messages are few: 3,798 in the author's corpus of 1.37
million records, 1,330 in its largest project. The cheap model can screen all of them for a
few cents.

## The changes

In the order they were made, each measured before the next:

1. **The sweep.** Every message the person wrote in the scope (the newest 4,000 at most) is
   screened against the question by the cheap model, in the order written, forty at a time,
   and the relevant ones join the records read. The check for later changes then screens every
   later message of the person's instead of searching for strings.
2. **A sample for the plan, a second plan when nothing is found, and what was not read.** The
   plan model sees forty of the person's messages, spread over the scope, before it chooses
   the strings. When no record holds any string, it chooses others once. The result reports
   the sweep's counts, which sessions in the scope the records read come from, and, for a
   string held by more records than were taken, the time before which none was read.
   `--export <path>` writes what was read.
3. **Whole sessions, and a digest.** With `--cwd`, the scope is every message of the sessions
   that have a message in that directory, also those written while the session was elsewhere
   (an app moves a session between folders: a question about this repository missed the
   message that names it, written in a scratch folder). The person's relevant messages beyond
   what the answer reads with their context go to it in one line each, at most 300.
4. **Fixes from a code review** of the above (a record the sweep screened was not counted as
   found by a string, a failed sweep chunk kept its first third as relevant, the completeness
   statements held when the sweep was cut at its limit), and the sweep on by default, with
   `--no-sweep`.

person's messages a project) the strings find most of what there is, so the default models
gain little; the mistakes were one each in three setups, a different case each time.

## On the author's history

Six questions whose answers the author's agent established by reading every message the
person wrote in the sessions concerned (four about this repository's development, one about
a game project, one about plans for a research feature), with the key points written before
any answer was seen. Four setups on change 1, one run each, judged by the author's agent from
a packet with the setups hidden; the corpus is 3.45 GB, 1,342 logs.

| Setup | Key points made | Answers with a mistake | Spent on the six |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.3.1, default models | 73% | 0 of 6 | $1.90 |
| Sweep (change 1), default models | 71% | 1 of 6 | $1.86 |
| 0.3.1, `--quick` | 58% | 0 of 6 | $0.13 |
| Sweep (change 1), `--quick` | 71% | 0 of 6 | $0.24 |

Change 1 alone did not help the default models here, and two of its answers showed why.
The question about this tool's name scored 2.5 of 4 in every setup: the message in which the
person explains the name was written while the session worked in a scratch folder, outside
the `--cwd` bound, so neither the strings nor the sweep saw it. And the broad question about
the person's standing rules (change 1's answer: 0.5 of 4, against 1.5 for 0.3.1) had the sweep
judge 469 of 909 messages relevant, of which the answer read 57, the newest with the highest
score, and lost the rule the question was about. Change 3 answers both: with whole sessions
the name question scored 4 of 4, the person's own explanation quoted; with the digest (1,072
messages in scope, 578 judged relevant, 300 given in one line each, 234 left out) the rules
question scored 2.5 of 4, at $0.58 and 227 s. Those two were asked again with change 3 only;
the other four were not.

## The build as committed

After the review fixes and with the sweep on by default, the forty-two cases were asked once
more with `--quick` as a check that nothing broke: all forty-two answered, $0.18 in all,
215 of 216 quotes verified, 55 of the 57 records the cases name cited. These answers were not
judged for their key points.

## What the numbers do not show

- One run each. The strings a plan chooses vary, and so does the screen.
- The made-up logs stay small. A project with a thousand of the person's messages (the
  author's largest has 1,330) is where the strings' caps bite and the sweep's digest is cut.
- The judging of the default run on the thirty, of the twelve, and of the author's history was
  by the agent that made the changes, from packets that hide the setup. The first sweep run
  was judged blind by a separate subagent.
- The six questions about the author's history are in Japanese about Japanese logs, as the
  evaluation's cases mostly are.
