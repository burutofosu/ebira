# 2026-10-20: how far the records get, and the agent's words around a message

0.4.0 screens every message of the person's in the scope. What it hands the answer is still
chosen in steps, and each step can drop what a question needs: the screen sees a long
message's head and tail only, a short reply ("go with that") alone, the agents' records only
the newest 40 for each string, and the answer gets the best 40 and a digest of at most 300
of the person's relevant messages. These measurements ask, for each step, how often a record
the question should cite is lost there, and whether a different way of doing the step keeps
it.

## How

- **The funnel.** `ask --evidence-only` stops before the answer and writes what it would
  read, and what the steps before it found, screened, and judged. `evals/run.py --evidence`
  places each record a case cites at the furthest step it reached: read with its context,
  shown in the digest, judged relevant but not handed over, screened and judged not
  relevant, found but not screened, or missed. A run costs about two US cents a question.
- **The same strings.** Every setup searched the strings that a planned run of 0.4.0
  searched in its first round (`--terms-from`); each run's own draft chose its second
  round. Two runs of 0.4.0 with the same strings measure the noise.
- **Twelve stress cases** (`evals/stress.py`), each straining one step: six short
  agreements to an agent's proposal (in three, the person never names the matter, and
  sixty newer agent records hold the proposal's words), a 2,417-character pasted
  spec with its points in the middle, 24 restatements of three rules among some 660 design
  remarks, and two causes an agent found months ago under fifty and more newer agent
  records holding the same words. With the forty-two cases before them, 54 cases cite 108
  records.

The cost is of the steps before the answer, with the strings given (the planned run also
chose them). Every setup handed over all 57 records of the forty-two earlier cases; the
differences are in the stress cases:

- **A short agreement nothing else names.** In one of the three hard cases, 0.4.0 missed
  both records in all three runs: the screen saw "そうしよう" after "続けて", and the
  proposal was not among the newest agent records. In the other two, the plan's strings
  ("から作", "消し") found the proposals. Showing the screen the agent's words around each message
  (`--sweep-context`: the last 120 characters of the reply before, the head and tail of the
  turn's last reply) found the agreement, and the proposal with it. Choosing the agents'
  records across months by the rarity of the strings they hold (`idf`), or screening all of
  them (`all`), found the proposal itself. The three easier agreements, where the person's
  question before the proposal names the matter, were handed over by every setup.
- **The middle of a long message.** The screen reads a long message's first 400 and last
  200 characters, and the answer its first 1,200 and last 600. The spec's first point,
  1,095 characters in, reached the answer in every setup; the second, 1,330 characters in,
  only when the screen read the message in overlapping pieces of 500 characters
  (`--sweep-chunks`) and the answer was shown the piece judged relevant.
- **Many relevant messages.** Every run judged all 24 restatements relevant; the loss was
  in handing them over. 0.4.0 hands over the best 40 and a digest of 300, the earliest and
  the latest halves when there are more (370 to 510 were relevant): in eight runs it handed
  over 17 to 24 of the 24. Handing over the highest scores first, one session at a time,
  identical messages once, within 36,000 characters (`--delivery spread`) handed over all 24
  in all eight of its runs, with about a tenth more text for the answer.
- **Old causes under newer records.** Every setup handed over both causes and the
  messages around them. In the planned run, the strings the draft suggested for the second
  round (the broker setting's name, the data file's name) found them.

## Results on the author's history

Six questions whose answer is an agent's report in the Ebira project (the result of a
labeling experiment, why an improvement was not taken in, the checks of four review points,
why an old migration was left alone), over the records before these measurements began. 0.4.0
handed over all six reports with their context, and so did `idf`, `all`, `--turn-replies`,
and `--sweep-context` with `--turn-replies`: the strings the plan chose were specific
enough. Per question, without the plan: 0.4.0 $0.022, `idf` $0.022, `all` $0.035,
`--turn-replies` $0.023, context and turns $0.029. The screen of the person's 158 messages
cost 0.4 cents a question, and 0.9 with their context.

## The answers

Handing over a record is what an answer needs, not proof that it gets better, so the
answers were compared too: 0.4.0 and the three ways together (`--sweep-context`,
`--sweep-chunks`, `--delivery spread`), with the same strings, one answer each, judged
without knowing which setup wrote which (as evals/JUDGE.md says).

| | 0.4.0 | The three ways |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 54 made-up questions: key points made | 95% | 100% |
| Answers with a mistake | 2 | 0 |
| Cost of the 54 answers | $2.91 | $2.98 |
| 6 questions on the author's history: key points made | 89% | 83% |
| Answers with a mistake | 0 | 0 |
| Cost of the 6 answers | $2.44 | $2.41 |

On the made-up logs, the answers differed where the funnel did. 0.4.0 said nothing was
decided about the agreement nothing else names, and about the spec's second point, which it
had read with the middle cut out; it named the three repeated rules without counting them.
The three ways answered all three, counting the rules nine, eight and seven times. On one
earlier question about a repeated rule, they gave half a point more; the other 41 earlier
questions scored the same.

On the author's history, the answers differed in details: 0.4.0 did better on three
questions, by half a point to a point, and the three ways on one, by half a point. One run of each cannot tell that apart from the
noise: an earlier comparison saw a question's score move by thirty points between runs of
the same setup. These six questions also strain little of what the ways fix: their scope
holds 158 of the person's messages. Seeing the agent's words changes what the screen
judges: on the question about Ebira's name, it judged 3 messages relevant instead of 24,
and the answer still made every point.

So the three ways are now the default: each fixes a loss measured where it occurs,
together they lost nothing on the made-up logs, and they cost the same.
`--no-sweep-context`, `--no-sweep-chunks`, and `--delivery current` keep the earlier ways.

The measurements cost about $18 of API use in all, the judged answers $7.33 of it. The
reversal check was a quarter of the funnel runs' cost; a funnel of the first steps alone
can skip it.

## `said --context`

The same lesson without the API: a short reply means nothing alone, for an agent reading
`said` as for the screen. Of the person's 3,802 messages in the author's corpus, 335 are of
twelve characters or fewer ("じゃあそうして" five times). With `--context`:

| | Messages | With the agent's reply before | With a reply in their turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All | 3,802 | 3,700 | 3,553 |
| Twelve characters or fewer | 335 | 332 | 281 |

A page of thirty took 0.92 seconds, against 0.91 without; every message of the corpus with
its context, 2 to 5 seconds. Before Codex's memory citations were left out, they were the
end of about one in seven Codex replies shown. Identical messages are rare in that corpus
(58 of 3,802), so showing each once would save little in `said`.
